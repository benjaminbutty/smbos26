# ADR-047 — Ordinary Records are progressively completable

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 25 August 2026

## Context

An owner often knows that a Company, Person, Opportunity or Follow-up exists
before all of its business details are known. Treating every configured
`Field.required` flag as a precondition for the Record itself made ordinary
Table, drawer and generic creation more cumbersome than a spreadsheet.

## Decision

An ordinary active graph Record may exist with omitted business Properties.
The graph trigger still applies authoritative Field defaults and still rejects
unknown, malformed, or newly introduced/changed archived values. It deliberately
does not turn an omitted Field into a Record-existence failure; unchanged
historical values on later-archived Fields remain readable while the rest of a
Record is completed.

`field_definitions.required` now means contextual completion where a trusted
experience or capability says it matters. Configured Forms continue to enforce
their required visible Properties. Public preorder, Booking and other
specialised capability transactions retain their own atomic validation; a
generic progressive Record cannot bypass them.

Direct Table creation captures the primary value the owner knows now. Normal
Table editing can add, replace or clear ordinary values later. Defaults remain
server owned and are shown as ordinary values rather than fabricated browser
input.

## Consequences

- Existing configurations gain progressive ordinary Record creation without a
  mass Field migration or owner reconfiguration.
- A configured Form may legitimately demand Email while the corresponding
  Person Record may exist without Email.
- Configuration Versions and rollback continue to govern definitions; they
  never delete or rewind operational Records.
- PostgreSQL remains the single validator for tenant, Field activity, type and
  default rules. This is not a client-side bypass.

## Exclusions

This does not introduce draft Records, a new completeness primitive, a generic
public Record endpoint, or a relaxation of capability-specific transaction
invariants.
